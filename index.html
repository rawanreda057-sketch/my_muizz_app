✦ About the Project ✦

مشروع التخرج Heritage Meets Geomatics

A graduation project bridging ancient Islamic heritage with modern GIS technology

The Vision

This graduation project applies Geomatics and GIS (Geographic Information Systems) to document, analyze, and visualize the cultural and architectural heritage of Al-Muizz Street — one of the world's most significant historic urban corridors.

Al-Muizz Street stretches approximately 1 kilometer through the heart of Fatimid Cairo, lined with over 30 major monuments spanning a thousand years of Islamic architecture.

Our goal is to create an interactive digital experience that makes this heritage accessible globally, combining spatial data, historical records, and advanced visualization tools.

  • ⬡ Spatial data collection & drone photogrammetry
  • ⬡ GIS-based monument mapping & analysis
  • ⬡ 3D visualization of historic structures
  • ⬡ Cultural heritage preservation documentation
  • ⬡ Interactive web-based public presentation

✦ Key Monuments ✦

المعالم التاريخية Landmarks of Al-Muizz

Explore the magnificent mosques, madrasas, gates, and palaces that line this ancient boulevard

City Gate
باب زويلة

Bab Zuweila

The last surviving southern gate of medieval Cairo, its twin minarets dominate the skyline. Once used for public executions, now a symbol of the city's resilience.

Built: 1092 CE · Fatimid
Mosque
مسجد الحاكم بأمر الله

Mosque of Al-Hakim

One of the most important Fatimid mosques in Cairo, with its distinctive cylindrical minarets. Named after the eccentric Fatimid Caliph Al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah.

Built: 990–1013 CE · Fatimid
Complex
مجمع قلاوون

Qalawun Complex

A magnificent 13th-century complex comprising a mausoleum, madrasa, and hospital (maristan). Its ablaq stonework and intricate stucco are masterworks of Mamluk art.

Built: 1284–1285 CE · Mamluk
Historic House
بيت السحيمي

Beit El-Suhaymi

A beautifully preserved Ottoman-era merchant's house featuring the iconic mashrabiya wooden screens, inner courtyards, and a central fountain — a window into domestic life.

Built: 1648 CE · Ottoman
Mosque
مسجد السلطان برقوق

Mosque of Sultan Barquq

The first Circassian Mamluk mosque built in Cairo's old city, notable for its elegant facade, twin minarets, and the tomb chamber featuring Quran recitation for eternity.

Built: 1384–1386 CE · Mamluk
Sabil-Kuttab
سبيل وكتّاب

Sabil-Kuttab of Abd el-Rahman Katkhuda

A two-story Ottoman structure combining a public water fountain (sabil) on the ground floor with a Quran school (kuttab) above — a cornerstone of community welfare.

Built: 1744 CE · Ottoman
✦ Historical Evolution ✦

الجدول الزمني A Thousand Years of History

From the Fatimid founding to the modern era — the unbroken story of Al-Muizz Street

969 CE Fatimid Conquest

Caliph Al-Muizz li-Din Allah marches the Fatimid dynasty into Egypt. Cairo (Al-Qahira) is founded as the royal enclave.

The City is Born

The main street of the new city becomes the ceremonial artery connecting Bab al-Futuh in the north to Bab Zuweila in the south.

Al-Hakim's Mosque

Work begins on the enormous Mosque of Al-Hakim at the northern end of the street, completing the Fatimid monumental axis.

990–1013 Late Fatimid Era

The street cements its religious and political importance, lined with Fatimid palaces that would later be demolished by Saladin.

1170s Ayyubid Period

Saladin dismantles the Fatimid palaces, repurposing the street for new administrative and religious functions under Sunni orthodoxy.

Ayyubid Transformation

Madrasas and khanqahs (Sufi lodges) begin to proliferate along the street, replacing Fatimid Shia institutions.

The Golden Mamluk Age

The street reaches its architectural peak. Qalawun, Al-Nasir Muhammad, Barquq — each sultan builds a magnificent complex along Al-Muizz.

1250–1517 Mamluk Sultanate

Cairo becomes the most important city in the Islamic world. Al-Muizz Street is its beating cultural and commercial heart.

1517–1798 Ottoman Era

Ottoman governors add sabils, kuttabs, and houses. The mashrabiya wooden screen becomes the defining domestic architectural element.

Ottoman Layering

Beit El-Suhaymi and other great merchant houses are built. Commercial life thrives around Khan el-Khalili nearby.

UNESCO Recognition

Historic Cairo is designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site, globally recognizing the extraordinary density of Islamic monuments.

1979 Modern Heritage

Al-Muizz Street is described by UNESCO as "an open-air museum" — the highest density of medieval Islamic monuments on Earth.

2008–Present Restoration & Digital Era

Major restoration project reopens Al-Muizz to pedestrians. Digital documentation and GIS mapping projects begin preserving heritage for future generations.

Our Project

This Geomatics graduation project contributes to the digital heritage preservation effort, creating interactive GIS maps and spatial analyses of the street.

✦ Historical Landmarks ✦

المعالم الأثرية Monuments Along the Street

The living museum — every step reveals a new era of Islamic civilization

Mosque
مسجد الأقمر

Al-Aqmar Mosque

One of Cairo's oldest surviving Fatimid mosques, renowned for its intricate stone facade with floral and stalactite carvings — the first mosque in Egypt with decorated stonework.

1125 CE · Fatimid
Complex
مجمع السلطان الناصر محمد

Al-Nasir Muhammad Complex

Built by the most prolific Mamluk builder, this complex features the famous ablaq stonework and a magnificent portal imported from a Crusader church in Acre, Palestine.

1295–1304 CE · Mamluk
Mosque & Tomb
مسجد الغوري

Al-Ghouri Mosque & Mausoleum

The last great Mamluk construction in Cairo, built by Sultan Al-Ghouri just years before the Ottoman conquest. Its distinctive red-and-white striped minaret is an iconic Cairo landmark.

1503–1505 CE · Mamluk
City Gate
باب الفتوح

Bab al-Futuh

The "Gate of Conquests" — the northern gate of Fatimid Cairo, built with massive limestone towers. A military and ceremonial marvel that still guards the ancient city walls.

1087 CE · Fatimid
Madrasa
المدرسة الكاملية

Al-Kamiliyya Madrasa

Built by Sultan Al-Kamil, this Ayyubid madrasa was among the first in Cairo to teach all four Sunni schools of law simultaneously, shaping Islamic jurisprudence for centuries.

1225 CE · Ayyubid
Mosque
مسجد المؤيد شيخ

Mosque of Al-Muayyad Sheikh

Built atop and into the fabric of Bab Zuweila, its two minarets crown the ancient gate's towers. The magnificent bronze doors were taken from Sultan Hassan's mosque.

1415–1420 CE · Mamluk

✦ Traditional Crafts ✦

الحرف التقليدية Artisans of Al-Muizz

Centuries-old crafts still practiced in the workshops and souqs lining this ancient street

🔨 النحاسيات

Coppersmithing (Al-Nahassin)

The ancient souk of the coppersmiths — Al-Nahhasin — runs along Al-Muizz. Artisans hammer intricate geometric and floral patterns into trays, pots, lanterns, and decorative bowls using traditional hand tools passed down through generations.

Souq Al-Nahassin
🪡 الخيامية

Khayamiya (Tent-Making)

Named after the Tent-Makers' Bazaar (Souq al-Khayamiyyin) at the south end of Al-Muizz, this craft produces richly patterned appliqué fabrics with intricate Islamic geometric and floral motifs, traditionally used for ceremonial tents and festival decorations.

Souq Al-Khayamiyyin
🏺 الخزف والفخار

Ceramics & Pottery

Fatimid Cairo was famed across the Islamic world for its lustrous ceramics. Today's artisans continue traditions of hand-thrown pottery with geometric and calligraphic decoration in earthen, blue-and-white, and turquoise glazes.

Khan el-Khalili Area
🪟 الزجاج المعشق

Stained Glass (Al-Zugag)

The art of colored leaded glass — seen in the windows of every great mosque along Al-Muizz. Craftsmen cut, color, and assemble geometric and floral patterns in glass, creating the luminous interiors for which Fatimid and Mamluk architecture is celebrated.

Monument Restoration Work
✍️ الخط العربي

Arabic Calligraphy

Considered the highest art form in Islamic culture, Arabic calligraphy adorns every monument along Al-Muizz — from Quranic inscriptions on mosque facades to manuscript illumination. Calligraphers in the Khan market still produce bespoke pieces in Thuluth, Naskh, and Kufic scripts.

Khan el-Khalili · Book Stalls
🪵 النجارة والمشربية

Mashrabiya Woodwork

The iconic turned-wood latticework screens of Cairo's Ottoman houses — seen on virtually every historic building along Al-Muizz. Carpenters hand-turn and assemble hundreds of wooden balls and rods without nails or glue, creating screens that cool air, filter light, and preserve privacy.

Woodworkers' Alley · Al-Muizz
